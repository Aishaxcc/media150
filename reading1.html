Week 1 Image Making Reflection

Photography and image making have honestly become a huge part of my life. I have been editing since I was around 12 years old. I basically taught myself everything through YouTube tutorials. I started off making YouTube banners and thumbnails for people online, which I would actually get paid for, and eventually I got really into editing videos with overlays, effects, transitions, and audio. Looking back now, I think that was the start of me realizing how much I loved visual media and storytelling.

Over time photography became something even more important to me. I love how images can capture emotions, personalities, and moments that would otherwise disappear. Even though photography can look realistic, every image still involves choices like lighting, framing, editing, and timing. Because of that, I think photography is not just about documenting reality but also about shaping how people see and understand it.

A lot of my relationship with image making comes from running my magazine, Jinxx. Jinxx is a platform for emerging artists and creatives, and honestly it has become my entire life. Working on editorials, styling, photography, creative direction, and events has taught me so much about both media and myself. It got to a point where I realized this was something I could not ignore anymore, which is why I ended up changing my major to Media Studies. Creative work stopped feeling like just a hobby and started feeling like my actual calling.

If I were trying to visually represent the world right now, I would probably focus on themes like internet culture, overstimulation, identity, and emotional disconnect. I think social media has changed the way people interact with each other and themselves. Everyone constantly documents their lives online, but sometimes those images feel more curated than real. I think combining photography with illustration, text, screenshots, or collage elements would reflect that feeling really well.

I also think visual media is powerful because people connect to images faster than they connect to long pieces of writing. A photograph can instantly create emotion or tell a story. That is part of why I love image making so much. It allows people to communicate feelings, ideas, and experiences in ways words sometimes cannot. Through my own work, I want to create images that feel emotional, personal, and reflective of the world people are currently living in.
